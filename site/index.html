Sources: ECMWF EC46 sub-seasonal ensemble mean, medium-range consensus of ECMWF IFS / NOAA GFS / DWD ICON, sea state from Open-Meteo Marine, and synoptic charts from charts.ecmwf.int — all via aegean_forecast.py. Each place uses its nearest sea grid cell, not land.

How the medium-range wind/temp/rain/gust numbers are calculated: each of the three models (ECMWF/GFS/ICON) is sampled over a 3×3 grid of points around every place — roughly 30×25 km, not a single GPS pin — because a boat sailing that spot experiences the whole patch of sea around it, not one exact point. Wind speed, direction, temperature, and rain are the mean across those 9 points per model, which smooths out single-cell grid noise. Gust is the max across those 9 points instead of the mean — gust is already a worst-case figure, so averaging it away would blunt a real local spike (a gap-wind gust one grid cell over, say) instead of surfacing it. The three models are then combined the same way on top of that: mean for wind/temp/rain/ direction, max for gust — so the gust shown is the strongest plausible gust anywhere in that patch, across all three models. That makes it a safety margin by design, deliberately higher than "the exact point forecast," not a bug.

Model disagreement: a place/day is flagged (⚠) when the three models' wind speeds spread by more than 6kt, or their directions by more than 45° while wind is at least 5kt. On a flagged day the wind cell shows the actual min–max span across models instead of one blended number (a mean of 6kt and 20kt would misrepresent both), and the full per-model breakdown always goes into runs.csv and into the data given to the AI summary above, so it can explain what the disagreement means for that day rather than silently averaging past it.

Ensemble/consensus wind smooths out individual fronts — read it as the week's regime, not a day-by-day forecast. EC46 (the coarsest, farthest-out tier: a single 51-member ensemble mean at ∼36 km resolution) and sea state (a single model, Open-Meteo Marine) are each still read at one point, not kernelled like the medium-range consensus above. Rows show the region's actual spread (sheltered vs. exposed spots), never a single averaged number, since this route crosses both. This page regenerates automatically on every run.
