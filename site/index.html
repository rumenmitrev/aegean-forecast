Sources: ECMWF EC46 sub-seasonal ensemble mean, medium-range consensus of , sea state from Open-Meteo Marine, and synoptic charts from charts.ecmwf.int — all via aegean_forecast.py. Each place uses its nearest sea grid cell, not land.

How the medium-range wind/temp/rain/gust numbers are calculated: each of the configured models () is sampled over a 3×3 grid of points around every place — roughly 30×25 km, not a single GPS pin — because a boat sailing that spot experiences the whole patch of sea around it, not one exact point. Wind speed, direction, temperature, and rain are the mean across those 9 points per model, which smooths out single-cell grid noise. Gust is the max across those 9 points instead of the mean — gust is already a worst-case figure, so averaging it away would blunt a real local spike (a gap-wind gust one grid cell over, say) instead of surfacing it. The models are then combined the same way on top of that: mean for wind/temp/rain/direction, max for gust — so the gust shown is the strongest plausible gust anywhere in that patch, across all of them. That makes it a safety margin by design, deliberately higher than "the exact point forecast," not a bug.

Model disagreement: a place/day is flagged (⚠) when the models' wind speeds spread by more than , or their directions by more than while wind is at least . On a flagged day the wind cell shows the actual min–max span across models instead of one blended number (a mean of two very different model reads would misrepresent both), and the full per-model breakdown always goes into runs.csv and into the data given to the AI summary above, so it can explain what the disagreement means for that day rather than silently averaging past it.

Sea state (wave height, period, direction) uses the same 3×3 kernel as wind, since a boat crossing a patch of sea can meet its roughest cell, not just the exact point: wave height is the kernel max (same worst-case reasoning as gust), period and direction are the kernel mean. Sea state is a single model (Open-Meteo Marine), so there's no further multi-model combine on top like wind has — the kernel is the only combining step here.

Ensemble/consensus wind smooths out individual fronts — read it as the week's regime, not a day-by-day forecast. EC46 (the coarsest, farthest-out tier: a single 51-member ensemble mean at ∼36 km resolution) is the one tier still read at a single point, with no kernel or per-model combine — it's already someone else's finished ensemble mean. Rows show the region's actual spread (sheltered vs. exposed spots), never a single averaged number, since this route crosses both. This page regenerates automatically on every run.

Poseidon regional forecast (independent, additive): a second, unofficial opinion from HCMR's (Hellenic Centre for Marine Research) own short-range Greek-seas model, shown alongside the ECMWF chart above — not a replacement for the tiers above it, and not folded into their kernel/consensus logic. Own model, own single point per place (no 3×3 kernel). Wind max is the day's highest 3-hourly sample, not a true gust — HCMR's model doesn't provide a separate gust figure, and its wave model gives no period either. This is not part of any documented, versioned public API (found by reading the public Poseidon map's own client code, the same request a browser makes) — it can change or vanish without notice, unlike every other source described above, and some coastal spots simply have no wave data (a masked/land grid cell in HCMR's model, shown as “–” rather than a real reading).
